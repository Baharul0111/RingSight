# RingSight — Goal

## The goal

RingSight is an investigation assistant for the people inside a bank or payment company who have to decide whether a flagged account is part of a money-laundering ring. It is Baharul Islam's entry for the WeMakeDevs × FalkorDB "Graph Hacks" hackathon (Track 01 "Agents That Act on Connected Data", also naming Track 02 "Agent Memory and Coordination"), submitted by Sunday 18 October 2026, 23:00 IST, and it is designed so it can later be shown to real banks and fintechs.

An investigator opens one web page, types a question in plain words — "Is ACC-4471 part of a ring, and how does the money move?" — and watches the assistant work. The assistant never invents a database query and never guesses: it chooses from a fixed menu of pre-written questions it is allowed to ask the connected data (accounts, the people who own them, the devices and phones they share, every transfer with its date and amount, and everything learned in earlier sessions), follows the connections several steps deep, and shows the exact chain it walked. Every answer is one of four honest kinds: "here is the path"; "I already knew this from an earlier session, learned on this date"; "nothing connects these within the depth or the data we hold — which is not the same as clean"; or "that account does not exist, did you mean one of these?".

It then proposes what to do — flag the account, recommend a hold, link it to a case, escalate, or draft the report a regulator expects — but nothing happens until a named person clicks approve or deny and writes a reason. A hold is only ever a recommendation to the Principal Officer; the assistant can never change an account's status itself. What it learns and what the human decided are remembered for the next session. All the data is synthetic (made up by a generator), nothing real is filed anywhere, and the whole demo runs on free hosting with a daily spending cap so it cannot run up a bill. Take the connected data store away and the assistant cannot answer, remember, propose, approve, or even say which payment rails are missing — that is the point.

## What it must do

1. **Run live on the internet and locally.** There is a public web address that opens the investigator page, plus a one-command local setup that starts the data store, loads the synthetic data and starts the app. The README's first line gives the live address and the judge's access key. A cold start may take about a minute and the README says so.

2. **Hold everything in one connected graph per bank.** Accounts, people, devices, phones, addresses, merchants, banks, payment rails, transfers, alerts, restrictions, the assistant's findings, its proposals, the humans' decisions, cases and report drafts all live in one FalkorDB graph named after the bank. A second tiny bank graph exists only to show that one bank can never see another's data.

3. **Generate realistic synthetic data on demand.** A generator creates about 25,000 accounts and their transfers over 180 days on six payment rails, including legitimate busy accounts (merchants, payroll, exchanges) so the assistant can tell a busy honest account from a ring. It plants 35 laundering rings across seven known patterns (fan-in, fan-out, cycles, bipartite, stacks, scatter-gather, gather-scatter), shared devices and phones inside rings, three pairs of owner names spelt differently ("Laxmi"/"Lakshmi"), three restricted accounts, sixty alerts, two days of mismatched feed counts on one rail, and twenty transfer memos that contain hidden instructions. The answer key for the planted rings is kept in files outside the graph so the assistant can never read it. The loaded graph must stay under 40 MB; the README prints the measured sizes.

4. **Reload itself automatically.** If the free data store restarts empty, the app notices, rebuilds the graph in the background from the generator and re-applies a saved snapshot of earlier findings, while the page says "warming up — reseeding, about N seconds". A stuck rebuild gives up after a deadline and says so.

5. **Answer from a fixed menu of sixteen typed questions, never a free-form query.** Twelve are built now: find an account/person/merchant by id or fuzzy name; recall earlier findings with the date learned; profile an account against its peers; show its neighbourhood 1 to 4 hops out within a time window; detect fan-in/fan-out with the drain afterwards; detect timed cycles of 3–5 hops with decaying amounts; detect scatter-gather and gather-scatter mule patterns; find the shortest money paths between two accounts; list a ring's members from shared identifiers or money communities; rank hub accounts by importance with legitimate hubs set aside; find accounts linked by shared devices, phones, addresses or owners (ignoring identifiers used by too many accounts); check restrictions on an account; and report which payment rails and days are loaded or missing. Every one has a human-written query and typed, bounded inputs; every id is checked to exist before anything runs; every question is answered "as of" a chosen moment so later transfers never leak in. A money-flow (max-flow) question, a backlog view and a queue view are documented for later, not built.

6. **Be reachable by any MCP tool as proof.** The fourteen read-only questions are published at a standard MCP address with an access key printed in the README, so a judge can connect MCP Inspector or Claude Code and list and call them. The two writing abilities (record a finding, propose an action) exist only inside the running app and are never published.

7. **Use the graph's own algorithms as tools.** Shortest paths, weakly-connected components, label propagation, PageRank and sampled betweenness are run inside FalkorDB; the global ones are precomputed when the data loads and read as properties, with "computed at" shown. The trace makes it visible which algorithm ran.

8. **Return the path with every answer.** Each answer shows one of the four outcome badges, the exact path as a hop list (for example ACC-4471 → DEV-f31c → ACC-10477 → PER-2291 → ACC-55820), the same path highlighted on a graph picture with money direction animated, and an evidence table of every transfer cited (date in IST, amount in rupees, from, to, rail, which question produced it). "Not in the graph" always carries the reason (no path within depth, rail not loaded, window not loaded, account isolated) and the banner "Not in the graph ≠ nothing wrong".

9. **Show its work as it happens.** A streaming trace lists every question asked: its name, its inputs, how long it took, how many rows came back, the outcome, and whether any text was flagged. The last line shows the model used, tokens, cache hits, cost in dollars, the judge's verdict and the verifier's result.

10. **Never state a name or number that is not in the evidence.** Before anything is shown, a checker confirms every account/person/device/transfer id, every amount (understanding ₹, lakh and crore) and every date in the sentence exists in the rows returned. If not, a plain sentence built from the rows is shown instead, with a "verifier replaced" chip. A second, separate model pass sees only the evidence rows and says whether they support, weakly support or contradict the conclusion; a weak "close this" is withdrawn and turned into "escalate".

11. **Treat an empty result as a reason to look harder, never as innocence.** A proposal to close or clear an account is refused unless at least two non-empty checks found nothing suspicious. A hold can only be proposed when a multi-step pattern (cycle, ring, shared identifiers, mule pattern, fan pattern) is in the evidence, never on a single transfer.

12. **Propose actions and wait for a named human.** The assistant can propose: flag, recommend a hold, link to a case, escalate, draft a US-style suspicious-activity report, draft an Indian suspicious-transaction report, or propose closing. Each becomes a pending card showing targets, evidence paths, any restriction already in force on the target (in red), and a draft preview. Approve, deny or modify each requires a written reason of at least 20 characters; the decision records who decided, their role and when. Approving a hold sends a recommendation to the Principal Officer and never touches the account itself; approving a report draft requires the Principal Officer role. Nothing is ever deleted — only superseded.

13. **Remember across sessions.** Findings are stored with the dates they were true and the date they were recorded, who wrote them (assistant or analyst) and whether a human reviewed them. Every new question first reads prior findings about the accounts involved and shows "answered from memory, learned on <date>, reviewed: yes/no" when it applies. An analyst can mark a finding wrong, which creates a superseding finding. Unreviewed assistant findings are clearly labelled and at most five of them are fed back into a new session.

14. **Resist planted instructions.** Text that comes from the data (transfer memos, names, earlier findings) is capped, wrapped as untrusted, scanned for instruction-like content and flagged in the trace; it is never allowed to act as an instruction. The assistant's own proposed text is refused if it contains role-mimicking or "ignore previous / skip approval" phrases, with the offending words named so it can rephrase, while honest wording like "recommend the Principal Officer hold the account" is accepted. Names and memos are scrubbed before any text reaches the model; the model only ever sees ids.

15. **Know who is asking and keep tenants apart.** Each access key maps, on the server, to a person, a role (analyst or Principal Officer) and a bank; the bank's graph is chosen from the key, never from anything the page sends. Every pending action, decision and case has an unguessable id that only resolves inside the caller's own bank graph; an automated test proves that key A cannot touch bank B's items. The page asks for the key once and never has one baked in.

16. **Never spend more than allowed.** Before any model is called: the Anthropic Console spending limit is the outer wall; a kill switch (halt, or disable the model) can stop everything instantly; a daily cap of $8 per bank stops further model calls for the day; at most three investigations run at once and six graph queries; each key and each visitor is rate-limited; and each investigation is capped at ten tool calls, about $0.25 and 60 seconds. When halted, out of budget or the model is down, the page switches to a "run a tool" form where a human can still run any of the fourteen read-only questions and see the path, with no AI involved.

17. **Stay honest when things break.** A model outage, a database restart, a timed-out query, a budget exhausted, too many requests, or a halted tenant each show a clear, plain message (never a stack trace) and the tool form keeps working. Every outside call has a timeout and limited retries. A health page reports whether the graph is loaded, its size, the pending count, today's spend, the engine version, and whether the model can actually be reached.

18. **Stay awake and tell you if it falls over.** Two free scheduled pingers and a free uptime monitor keep the live app and the free database awake through judging week and e-mail Baharul if the health page fails; a daily automated question proves answers still work after a reload.

19. **Export the regulator's case file.** For an approved report draft, the page renders a US-style narrative (who, what, when, where, why, how, with each transfer's date and amount listed, no tables) or an Indian-style report (grounds-of-suspicion tags, narration with a character count under 20,000, the transaction-sheet type per rail), both downloadable as text and as a data file, each with a footer stating which rails and dates were loaded when the case was worked, and the Indian format facts labelled as not yet verified against the official document.

20. **Prove itself with automated checks that are committed to the repo.** Local, no-model tests: recall of planted ring members per pattern and hub precision with legitimate hubs present; that no future-dated transfer ever leaks into an "as of" answer; that unknown ids and unloaded rails abstain correctly; that actions end up in the right state (hold refused on one transfer, close refused on empty evidence, restrictions echoed, guard refusals naming the pattern, honest hold/close wording accepted, decisions recorded, no account status ever written, the cross-bank access test); and that the twenty planted memo injections are flagged and cannot be written into memory. Model tests on a local copy, never on the live graph: path correctness at depth 1/2/4, abstention when ring edges are deleted, the twenty injections end to end with zero unapproved effects; memory and latency tests if time allows. Results are committed as files and summarised in the README. Detection numbers are labelled "synthetic, demo-only".

21. **Ship everything the hackathon asks for.** A public GitHub repository (MIT for RingSight's own code, with the FalkorDB SSPL notice and the AMLSim Apache-2.0 attribution); a README with the live address, local setup, the graph data model with measured sizes, every query and algorithm call inline, the tool catalogue and approval flow, the four shapes, why there is no free-form query generation, memory, safety and kill switch, the eval numbers, operations and runbook ("if the demo is down"), the regulatory mapping and pilot posture, the scale plan, the AI-assistance disclosure sentence, licences and data statement, honest limitations, and the blog link; a 3-minute screen-recorded demo video following the script (problem → the live run with depth 1 then depth 4 revealing the hidden exit account → approve a case and deny a hold with reasons → the nodes visible in FalkorDB Browser → memory, unknown account and "not in the graph" → the pre-planted injected memo being neutralised → honest limits); the tracks named as "Track 01 (primary) · Track 02 (memory)"; and a draft blog post for the side prize.

22. **Be fast enough to demo and honest about speed.** Depth-4 investigations should complete in about 15 seconds on the live free tier; the measured p50/p95 are published, not promised. The page works on a laptop browser, shows empty/loading/error states, and prints rupees from integer paise.

## How we know it is finished

A fresh clone with the `.env` filled in starts with one command locally (data store, synthetic data, app) and the live address answers the demo question at depth 1 and at depth 4, proposes, accepts an approval with a reason, remembers in a new session, says "unknown" for a made-up account and "not in the graph — rail not loaded" for a cash question, refuses the planted memo's instruction, cannot be spent past its caps, exposes exactly the fourteen read-only questions over MCP and nothing more, and keeps working as a plain tool form when the model is switched off. Every behaviour above is proven by automated tests that pass, with the results committed, and the README, video script, disclosure sentence and blog draft exist in the repo. Two steps wait for Baharul's explicit go-ahead because they are public or spend money beyond the plan: the final hackathon submission form, and any raise of the daily spending cap or the Console spending limit.
